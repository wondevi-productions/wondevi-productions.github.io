How much should a mobile mechanic charge per hour?

There's no single right number. There is a minimum number for your business, and it's usually higher than people think. Here's how to find it in five steps.

1. Add up a full year of costs

Use your bank statements, not your memory. Mobile operators commonly miss these:

  • Van: fuel, registration, insurance, servicing and tyres, finance or depreciation
  • Public liability and tool insurance
  • Diagnostic scan tool and workshop data subscriptions, often $150-$300 a month together
  • Tool purchases and replacement
  • Phone and data, booking and accounting software, website and ads
  • Accountant, bank and card fees, licences and training, workwear and PPE

For a typical one-van operation this lands somewhere between $30,000 and $40,000 a year before you pay yourself.

2. Decide what you need to earn

Put a real wage on yourself, the amount you'd need if you employed someone to do your job. Profit comes on top of that, not instead of it.

3. Count your billable hours honestly

Weeks worked x hours per week x utilisation. Allow for holidays, sick days and quiet weeks (46 weeks is common), and remember utilisation: if you're on the road 45 hours a week but bill 29, your utilisation is 65%.

4. Divide

(Costs + wage) / billable hours = break-even rate. Every hour you charge below this costs you money, however busy you are.

5. Add margin

Divide the break-even rate by (1 - target margin). At $89 break-even and a 25% margin, that's $119/hr. This margin pays for slow months, replacing the van, and growth.

Common mistakes

  • Copying a competitor's rate. They may be underpricing too, or have far lower costs.
  • Ignoring travel time. An hour driving is an hour you can't bill. Recover it with a call-out fee or travel charge (see travel and call-out fees).
  • Thin parts markup. Small parts often cost more to source than you make on them (see the parts markup guide).
  • Never checking quoted vs actual hours. If brake jobs keep taking 3 hours and you quote 2, your effective rate is a third lower than you think.

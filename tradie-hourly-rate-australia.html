How much should a tradie charge per hour in Australia?

Electricians, plumbers, mechanics, carpenters, HVAC techs and handymen all face the same trap: setting a rate by copying what others charge. Your costs and your billable hours are your own. Here's how to work out a rate that actually pays, for any trade.

The formula

Minimum hourly rate = (annual business costs + the wage you need) / billable hours per year. Then add your profit margin on top.

1. Business costs most sole traders forget

  • Ute or van: fuel, rego, CTP and comprehensive insurance, servicing, tyres, finance or depreciation
  • Public liability (and professional indemnity where your trade needs it), tool insurance
  • Licence renewals, white card and ticket refreshers, association memberships
  • Tools and test equipment, plus software subscriptions (quoting, accounting, scheduling)
  • Phone and data, website and ads, accountant and bank fees, workwear and PPE

2. The wage you actually need

As a sole trader there's no employer paying your super, sick leave or holidays. Build them into the wage you need from the business, along with income tax.

3. Billable hours, not hours worked

Travel between jobs, quoting, supplier runs, invoicing and call-backs eat into your day. Most mobile trades bill only 55-70% of the hours they work. At 46 weeks x 45 hours x 65%, that's about 1,345 billable hours a year.

4. Don't forget GST

If you're registered for GST, your hourly rate should be worked out ex GST and the 10% added on top. Quoting a GST-inclusive figure as if it were your rate quietly costs you a tenth of your income. Use the GST estimate for a single BAS period. It is a rough figure, not a lodged BAS.

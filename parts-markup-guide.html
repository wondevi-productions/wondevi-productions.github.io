What markup should you put on parts?

A flat 20-30% markup sounds fair, but it loses money on cheap parts and can price you out on expensive ones. Most profitable shops use tiered markup.

Why flat markup doesn't work

A $12 sensor at 25% earns you $3. Ordering it, collecting it and handling a return can easily take 20 minutes, which is worth $30-40 at a proper hourly rate. A $1,800 gearbox at 25% adds $450, which can lose you the job to someone quoting 15%.

A typical tiered markup table

Part cost (ex GST)MarkupExample
Under $5080-100%$25 cost -> $50 sell
$50 - $25040-50%$120 -> $180
$250 - $1,00025-30%$450 -> $585
$1,000+15-20%$1,500 -> $1,800

Adjust the bands to your trade and market, but keep the shape: higher percentages on small parts, lower on big-ticket items.

Track what you pay each supplier

Log every purchase for a month: supplier, part, cost and what you sold it for. Many operators find one supplier's trade price is consistently 10-15% higher than another's for the same parts. That's margin you can recover without charging customers more.

Markup vs margin

Markup is profit / cost. Margin is profit / sell price. A 50% markup is only a 33% margin. Quote with markup, but judge the job on margin.

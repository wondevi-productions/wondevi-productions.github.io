Used car inspection checklist: 15 checks before you buy

Most expensive used-car mistakes can be spotted in a careful inspection. Run through these 15 checks on any car you're serious about. If something fails, either negotiate the cost of the repair or walk away.

CheckWhat to look for
☐History checkRun a VIN history check (in Australia, a PPSR search) for money owing, write-offs and theft.
☐Cold startAsk the seller not to start the car before you arrive. Watch for smoke and listen for knocks on start-up.
☐Oil capMilky residue under the oil filler cap can mean a head gasket problem.
☐Panel gaps & paintUneven gaps or mismatched paint suggest crash repairs.
☐RustCheck wheel arches, sills, door bottoms and the boot floor.
☐TyresLook for uneven wear (alignment or suspension faults) and check the 4-digit date code.
☐LeaksLook under the engine and gearbox for fresh oil, coolant or fuel.
☐Water damageDamp carpet, a musty smell or silt in the boot are flood warning signs.
☐GearboxAutomatics should change smoothly, without slipping, flaring or thumping.
☐BrakesThe car should stop straight without pulsing or grinding.
☐SteeringNo pulling to one side and no vibration at highway speed.
☐Warning lightsAll lights should come on with the key, then go out once the engine starts.
☐Air conIt should blow cold within a couple of minutes.
☐Service historyThe logbook should be stamped at the right intervals and match the odometer.
☐Two keysReplacement keys can cost hundreds of dollars.

Bring these with you

  • A torch (for under the car and the engine bay)
  • Paper towel (to check oil and fluid colour)
  • Your phone (photos of faults, VIN and service book)
  • A friend - one of you talks to the seller while the other inspects

When to get a professional inspection

If you find anything serious, such as smoke, a slipping gearbox, structural rust, or money owing on the car, either get a mechanic's pre-purchase inspection before you pay or walk away. There is always another car.
